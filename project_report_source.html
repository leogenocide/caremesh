Simultaneously, conventional social media platforms—while ubiquitous—are fundamentally architected around engagement algorithms that inadvertently amplify sensationalism, unverified rumors, and uncoordinated relief efforts during crises.

This project report documents the design, engineering, and empirical evaluation of “A Social Coordination Network”. It is hoped that this documentation serves not only as an academic milestone for the degree curriculum but also as an actionable, open blueprint for resilient digital public infrastructure and community-first mutual aid networks.

1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 Overview of the Project

In recent decades, modern societies have faced an increasing frequency of localized disruptions, acute environmental crises, and structural socio-economic stresses. When crises strike—ranging from extreme weather anomalies and infrastructural outages to public health emergencies—the survival and resilience of communities hinge heavily on rapid, localized, and verifiable mutual aid. Historically, citizen response has been organized either through centralized municipal agencies or ad-hoc informal networks such as neighborhood groups, phone trees, and commercial social media platforms.

However, contemporary crisis coordination suffers from two distinct systemic failures. On one side, centralized municipal relief pipelines are frequently overwhelmed by communication bottlenecks, logistical latency, and bureaucratic overhead during the critical initial 72 hours of an emergency. On the other side, conventional commercial social media networks (such as microblogging feeds and messaging apps) are algorithmically optimized for engagement rather than truth, resulting in the rapid propagation of unverified rumors, panic, duplicate relief requests, and chaotic resource misallocations. In an emergency, an unverified rumor regarding water contamination or road blockage can divert life-saving resources away from areas of dire need.

To bridge this vital gap, A Social Coordination Network has been engineered as an epistemic, deliberative, and decentralized civic coordination platform. The project departs radically from existing social networking paradigms by replacing engagement-driven feeds with verifiable, evidence-grounded civic operations. The system establishes a structured digital public infrastructure where every community observation is anchored to empirical evidence, conflicting claims trigger automated contradiction detection audits, resources are matched with urgent needs via a transparent and explainable 5-factor scoring algorithm, and community initiatives are governed through structured deliberation with full decision traceability.

1.2 Objective and Scope

1.2.1 Objective

The primary objective of A Social Coordination Network is to engineer a resilient, high-speed, and epistemically grounded software platform that empowers self-organizing communities to verify critical crisis observations, coordinate emergency aid, and govern collective initiatives without dependence on brittle centralized cloud services.

Specific architectural and functional objectives include:

  • Epistemic Grounding & Claim Verification: To implement a rigorous evidence-linking subsystem where observations regarding crisis hazards, relief camps, or supply shortages must cite verifiable physical or digital evidence, enabling peer-audited claim verification and contradiction alerts.
  • Deterministic Multi-Factor Matching: To eliminate subjective bias and algorithmic opacity by designing an explainable, deterministic 5-factor scoring engine (evaluating Urgency, Geospatial Proximity, Skill Match, Availability, and Impact Potential) to pair community requests with verified resources.
  • Deliberative Plan Governance: To provide a structured 5-stage collaborative planning lifecycle (Idea → Proposal → Deliberation → Active Execution → Completed / Archived) featuring categorized critique incorporation, community consensus tracking, and immutable decision logs.
  • Living Institutional Memory: To automate post-action outcome evaluations that codify operational lessons, historical successes, and failure modes into persistent organizational knowledge for future disaster preparedness.
  • Sovereign & Zero-Cost Architecture: To implement a lightweight, zero-cloud-cost stack using embedded relational storage (SQLite with Write-Ahead Logging) and open-source geospatial mapping (Leaflet and OpenStreetMap), ensuring the platform can operate reliably in resource-constrained environments and local disaster hubs.

1.2.2 Scope

The functional and operational scope of A Social Coordination Network encompasses both urban neighborhood crisis mitigation and ongoing municipal mutual aid programs. The boundaries of the project scope are delineated as follows:

  • Geospatial Exploration & Mapping: Interactive, full-viewport mapping of observations, relief supply nodes, hazards, and community initiatives using client-side geospatial clustering and spatial bounding box queries.
  • Collaborative Mutual Aid Exchange: Comprehensive lifecycle tracking for relief requests and surplus resource offers, including multi-criteria filtering, contact privacy protections, and automated match recommendations.
  • Structured Civic Deliberation: Specialized plan authoring tools that enforce critique categorization (e.g., feasibility, safety, equity, budget), structured counter-arguments, and transparent decision audit histories.
  • Role-Based Administrative Governance: Administrative consoles for verified community coordinators and public moderators to audit dispute claims, enforce content quarantines, manage user reputation standings, and monitor system health.
  • Target Deployment Scope: The system is optimized for deployment on localized municipal edge servers, community mesh nodes, or standard virtual private servers, providing low latency and immediate accessibility across standard web browsers on desktop and mobile devices.

2.2 Problem Statement / Overall Description

During disasters and localized civic emergencies, communities face an acute information and coordination asymmetry:

  • Information Toxicity & Rumor Mills: Existing communication channels (e.g., WhatsApp groups, Twitter/X, Facebook feeds) intermix vital situational updates with unverified rumors, outdated distress calls, and malicious disinformation. Responders cannot easily distinguish between a currently collapsed bridge and a resolved obstruction from three days prior.
  • Resource Allocation Chaos: Relief supply distributions typically suffer from severe supply-demand mismatches—some neighborhoods receive triple shipments of bottled water while an adjacent area lacks basic baby formula or emergency power. Uncoordinated goodwill results in logistical paralysis.
  • Lack of Epistemic Traceability: When conflicting claims arise regarding road safety, medical triage availability, or water purity, conventional platforms offer no formal mechanism to cite provenance, chain of custody, or physical evidence.
  • Centralized Vulnerability & High Cloud Costs: Most crisis tech platforms rely on complex, expensive cloud-native architectures (microservices, distributed caches, hosted PostgreSQL clusters) that incur prohibitive operational costs and completely fail when regional internet connectivity degrades.

A Social Coordination Network resolves these challenges by introducing a decentralized, self-contained digital architecture that combines empirical observation-evidence graphs, deterministic multi-factor matching, and formal deliberative governance on an ultra-resilient, lightweight software stack.

2.8 Overview of Web Application

2.8.1 What is a Modern Web Application?

A modern web application is an interactive software system executed directly within the client's web browser environment, combining dynamic, component-driven user interfaces with decoupled, asynchronous backend services. Unlike traditional multi-page websites that require full-page reloads from the server on every user interaction, modern web applications utilize a Single Page Application (SPA) paradigm. In this architecture, the initial HTML shell, JavaScript bundles, and stylesheet assets are downloaded once. Subsequent user interactions dynamically re-render the view hierarchy and fetch granular data payloads via lightweight JSON over asynchronous HTTP REST channels.

2.8.2 Web System Architecture (Client-Server 3-Tier Pipeline)

A Social Coordination Network is structured around an ultra-efficient 3-Tier architectural pipeline:

  • Presentation Tier (Client): The React 19 SPA running on the user's browser, responsible for rendering views, managing client-side routing, handling UI state, and rendering the vector-based Leaflet geospatial canvas.
  • Application Logic Tier (Server): The Node.js and Express 5 REST API gateway, responsible for request authentication, input validation, execution of the 5-factor matching algorithm, contradiction auditing, and transaction orchestration.
  • Data Storage Tier (Embedded Relational): Better-SQLite3 running directly within the application process in Write-Ahead Logging (WAL) mode, guaranteeing ACID transactions, sub-millisecond query execution, and zero inter-process network latency.

2.8.3 Application Components

The system decomposes into four primary architectural subsystems:

  1. Epistemic Observation & Evidence Subsystem: Manages the ingestion, indexing, and validation of community observations, establishing relational links to tangible evidence artifacts (photos, official bulletins, test readings).
  2. Deterministic Matching Engine: Computes real-time affinity scores between community requests and available resources using a normalized 5-dimensional scoring model.
  3. Deliberative Action Planning Subsystem: Manages collective initiatives through a formal 5-stage proposal lifecycle with categorized critique tracking and immutable decision logging.
  4. Geospatial & Community Hub: Provides interactive spatial clustering of local assets, neighborhood readiness indexing, and community communication channels.

2.8.4 React Component Lifecycle & State Management

The frontend utilizes React 19's declarative component architecture. State is managed at three distinct granularities:

  • Local Component State (useState, useReducer): Encapsulates isolated transient UI interactions such as modal visibility, form input buffers, and filter toggles.
  • Shared Context State (NetworkContext): Provides global hydration of active session credentials, current user roles, system notifications, and cached core datasets across the entire component tree.
  • Lifecycle & Asynchronous Synchronization (useEffect, useMemo, useCallback): Coordinates API fetch cycles, throttles geospatial superclustering updates during map panning, and binds browser history navigation events.

2.8.5 Data Flow & Request-Response Pipeline

Communication between the presentation layer and the application tier follows strict RESTful conventions. All data exchanges are formatted as JSON payloads. When a user submits an observation or initiates a resource match, the client issues an asynchronous fetch() request with an Authorization: Bearer <JWT> header. The Express server passes the request through rate limiting and authentication middleware, executes parameter validation, performs the required SQLite transaction, and returns an HTTP status code accompanied by a structured JSON payload.

2.8.6 Asynchronous Operations & Event Loops

Both the Node.js backend and browser frontend rely on the JavaScript Event Loop concurrency model. In the Node.js server, non-blocking asynchronous I/O allows thousands of concurrent read requests to be processed seamlessly. For disk operations, Better-SQLite3 operates synchronously in C++, eliminating the overhead of asynchronous promise scheduling while WAL mode ensures concurrent readers never block active writers.

2.8.7 Security Features of the Web Application

Security is designed as a foundational pillar throughout the software architecture:

  • Stateless Cryptographic Authentication: JSON Web Tokens (JWT) signed with HMAC-SHA256 authenticate user requests without requiring server-side session state.
  • Robust Credential Hashing: Passwords are protected using BcryptJS with a salt work factor of 10, rendering rainbow table and brute-force attacks computationally infeasible.
  • Strict SQL Parameterization: Every database query is executed using SQLite prepared statements (e.g., db.prepare('SELECT * FROM users WHERE id = ?').get(id)), completely immunizing the platform against SQL Injection (SQLi) attacks.
  • CORS & Security Headers: Cross-Origin Resource Sharing (CORS) is configured to prevent unauthorized cross-domain exploitation, coupled with strict input validation.
  • Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Middleware intercepts sensitive routes (such as claim quarantines, dispute resolutions, and moderation actions) to verify administrative privileges.

2.8.8 Evolution and History of Social Coordination Platforms

The conceptual evolution of civic coordination tools spans three distinct generations:

  • First Generation (Ad-Hoc Forums & Bulletin Boards): Early platforms (e.g., Craigslist, mailing lists, early wiki portals) enabled basic posting of aid requests but lacked geospatial awareness, real-time status tracking, and verification mechanisms.
  • Second Generation (Commercial Social Networks & Crowdsourcing): Tools like Twitter/X, Facebook Crisis Response, and Ushahidi introduced geospatial crisis mapping and viral reach. However, their reliance on attention-maximizing engagement algorithms led to severe misinformation epidemics and lack of accountability.
  • Third Generation (Epistemic & Deliberative Coordination Networks): Represented by A Social Coordination Network, this modern generation synthesizes evidence-backed knowledge graphs, transparent deterministic algorithmic matching, and formal deliberative governance to provide reliable, tamper-resistant digital public infrastructure.

2.8.9 Core System Features

The Four Pillars of A Social Coordination Network:
  1. Epistemic Grounding: No observation stands in isolation. Every critical crisis report is linked to empirical evidence artifacts and audited for contradictions.
  2. Explainable 5-Factor Matching: Resource allocation is governed by an open, deterministic mathematical formula balancing Urgency, Proximity, Skill Match, Availability, and Impact.
  3. Deliberative Plan Governance: Community projects follow a structured consensus pipeline where critiques are formally categorized and addressed before activation.
  4. Living Institutional Memory: Automatic outcome evaluations harvest operational lessons from completed plans, strengthening community preparedness over time.

3. SYSTEM ANALYSIS DESIGN

3.1 Introduction

System Analysis is the process of examining a business, operational, or social situation with the intent of improving it through better methods and procedures. For A Social Coordination Network, system analysis focuses on dissecting the failure modes of current disaster response workflows, mapping information hand-offs between crisis actors, identifying data integrity bottlenecks, and formulating an optimal technological intervention.

The analysis demonstrates that disaster relief and community mutual aid are fundamentally distributed coordination problems that break down when communication networks are stressed. By understanding the operational constraints of community responders, the system analysis establishes a model that prioritizes zero-friction onboarding, empirical verification, and deterministic resource allocation.

3.2 Feasibility Study

A formal feasibility analysis was conducted across technical, economic/operational, and legal dimensions prior to full software engineering to ensure viability, sustainability, and real-world applicability.

3.2.1 Technical Feasibility

The technical feasibility study evaluated whether the proposed functionality could be implemented using current technologies, available hardware, and within the constraints of local disaster deployments:

  • Stack Maturity: React 19, Node.js, Express, and SQLite are battle-tested, mature open-source technologies with extensive global developer ecosystems and thorough documentation.
  • Low-Resource Footprint: Better-SQLite3 with Write-Ahead Logging (WAL) requires minimal RAM and zero background database server daemon overhead. It can run comfortably on a $5/month VPS or a lightweight localized edge server such as a Raspberry Pi 4.
  • Geospatial Viability: Leaflet 1.9 combined with Supercluster provides fluid 60-FPS geospatial marker clustering in the browser, capable of rendering thousands of concurrent spatial points without server-side GIS licensing costs (such as ESRI or Google Maps API charges).
  • Deterministic Matcher Complexity: The 5-factor scoring engine operates with linear time complexity O(N) relative to active resource listings, ensuring instantaneous sub-10ms calculation times on modest hardware.

Conclusion: The technical feasibility of the system is extraordinarily high, with zero reliance on speculative or proprietary black-box technologies.

3.2.2 Operational & Economic Feasibility

The operational and economic assessment evaluated user adoption barriers and long-term financial sustainability:

  • Economic Viability (Zero-Cost Operation): The entire technology stack is comprised of 100% free and open-source software (FOSS). There are no recurring database licensing fees, per-seat user costs, or cloud API billing cliffs. Municipalities or community collectives can host the entire system on existing civic infrastructure at virtually zero marginal cost.
  • Operational Usability: The user interface is engineered to adhere to modern accessibility and cognitive load minimization standards. During high-stress crises, users need simple, legible interfaces. Clear color tokens, spatial maps, and one-click request submission ensure high community adoptability.
  • Maintenance Simplicity: The database is stored in a single self-contained disk file (coordination.db), making system backups as trivial as copying a file, drastically reducing operational IT maintenance overhead.

Conclusion: The system is economically self-sustaining and operationally viable for grassroots organizations and municipal bodies alike.

3.2.3 Legal & Ethical Feasibility

The legal and ethical study examined compliance with data privacy regulations, user rights, and community safety:

4.3.3 Level 2 DFD (Verification & Matching Subsystems)

The Level 2 DFD drills into the operational core: the Deterministic Multi-Factor Matching Pipeline. When a citizen submits a mutual aid request, the engine executes five parallel evaluators:

  1. Urgency Evaluator (w₁ = 0.30): Categorizes temporal severity (Critical > High > Medium > Low).
  2. Geospatial Haversine Proximity Evaluator (w₂ = 0.25): Calculates physical travel distance d between coordinates and applies exponential spatial decay e-λd.
  3. Skill & Category Matcher (w₃ = 0.20): Evaluates tag overlap between required assistance and volunteer skill arrays.
  4. Temporal Availability Evaluator (w₄ = 0.15): Cross-references volunteer active schedules and provider operational hours.
  5. Historical Impact Score (w₅ = 0.10): Factorizes the provider's verified past fulfillment completion rate.
The Matching Equation:

Score = (0.30 × Urgency) + (0.25 × Proximity) + (0.20 × SkillMatch) + (0.15 × Availability) + (0.10 × Impact)

Every matched resource pairing is annotated with this exact breakdown, giving both parties complete auditability over why a match was recommended.

5. SYSTEM DESIGN

5.1 Introduction

System Design is the creative and technical phase that translates structural models into tangible data definitions, input interfaces, physical file formats, and screen presentations. While technical design establishes what data is stored and how it flows, system design defines how humans interact with the software, how input errors are trapped, how information is physically formatted on persistent disks, and how outputs are presented visually to support rapid, life-critical decision-making.

5.2 Input Design

Input design focuses on minimizing user cognitive load, eliminating data entry errors, and ensuring that observational telemetry submitted from the field is rigorous and verifiable.

  • Observation Submission Interface: Enforces structured category selection (Hazard, Medical Shortage, Shelter Capacity, Water/Food Distribution), automated geospatial GPS coordinate capture (or interactive map pin placement), neighborhood tagging, and mandatory attachment of at least one evidence artifact.
  • Evidence Attachment Form: Requires users to define evidence type (Physical Photo, Audio Recording, Official Municipal Dispatch, Sensor Reading), URL or asset identifier, author attribution, and a cryptographic/chain-of-custody provenance statement.
  • Mutual Aid Request / Resource Form: Enforces standardized resource unit types (e.g., liters, boxes, human hours), urgency level toggles, temporal validity periods, and privacy masking options.
  • Plan Critique Interface: Rather than unstructured comment boxes, critiques must be tagged under strict taxonomy categories: Feasibility, Safety, Resource Adequacy, Inclusivity, or Budgetary/Logistical.
  • Input Validation & Sanitization: Client-side validation prevents malformed inputs before dispatch, while server-side Express validators sanitize strings against Cross-Site Scripting (XSS) and enforce strict type schemas.

5.3 Physical Design

The physical design dictates how the data and assets are physically arranged on the host storage filesystem:

  • Single-File Database Architecture: The entire relational state is persisted in server/db/coordination.db (operating under SQLite 3.45+). This eliminates distributed consensus latency and allows single-command atomic backups.
  • Write-Ahead Logging (WAL): Concurrency is configured via PRAGMA journal_mode = WAL; and PRAGMA synchronous = NORMAL;. Changes are appended sequentially to coordination.db-wal before being checkpointed to the primary database file. This architecture allows simultaneous concurrent read operations without locking out writers.
  • Binary & Static Asset Storage: User avatars, incident photos, and evidence documents are decoupled from the relational database file, stored within a dedicated /public/uploads directory structure, and referenced via immutable content URLs.
  • B-Tree Indexing Strategy: Critical relational query paths are accelerated via specialized indexes: 
    CREATE INDEX idx_obs_author ON observations(author_id);
CREATE INDEX idx_obs_coords ON observations(lat, lng);
CREATE INDEX idx_req_urgency ON requests(urgency, status);
CREATE INDEX idx_res_category ON resources(category, status);
CREATE INDEX idx_plans_stage ON plans(stage);

7. LIMITATION OF THE PROJECT

While A Social Coordination Network provides a resilient, verifiable, and sovereign digital architecture for community crisis mutual aid, rigorous software engineering necessitates transparent identification of inherent system limitations and operational boundaries:

  • Local Embedded Database Write Concurrency Limits: The choice of Better-SQLite3 operating in WAL mode guarantees exceptional read throughput and zero infrastructure cost. However, because SQLite utilizes a single-writer locking architecture, extremely high-frequency concurrent write spikes (exceeding thousands of simultaneous write transactions per second during a massive regional cataclysm) could introduce write queue latency unless handled via queue throttling.
  • Internet Backhaul Dependency for Map Tiles: While the core application logic, database, and cached data function entirely offline within a local disaster relief Wi-Fi mesh, the high-resolution vector tile imagery relies on external OpenStreetMap tile servers unless an offline raster map tile pack has been pre-downloaded onto the deployment node.
  • Human Moderator Engagement in Contentious Disputes: The platform provides automated contradiction detection when conflicting observations are reported. However, the final epistemic resolution of deeply contentious real-world situations (e.g., assessing conflicting eyewitness accounts regarding water potable status) still requires active human judgement and verified community moderator arbitration.
  • Deterministic Matching Heuristic Simplicity: The 5-factor scoring engine ensures absolute auditability and fairness, completely preventing black-box algorithmic bias. Nonetheless, deterministic scoring operates purely on quantified metrics (distance, urgency, skill tags) and cannot intuitively infer nuanced, qualitative human emotional context or interpersonal dynamics without direct human coordinator oversight.
  • Cross-Instance Federation Boundaries: In its current iteration, each deployment functions as an autonomous, sovereign community instance. Adjoining municipalities running separate instances cannot currently cross-query mutual aid surpluses without manual coordination or future federated sync protocols.
8. CONCLUSION

Disaster relief and grassroots mutual aid represent some of the most critical, high-stakes coordination challenges in human society. When crises disrupt conventional public infrastructure, the survival and rapid recovery of communities depend on the availability of reliable, tamper-resistant, and verifiable information channels.

A Social Coordination Network successfully demonstrates that digital civic infrastructure can be engineered to prioritize truth over engagement, deterministic fairness over opaque algorithms, and local sovereignty over expensive cloud dependencies. By systematically implementing:

  1. Epistemic Grounding: Linking every observational report to tangible physical evidence artifacts and automating contradiction detection;
  2. Explainable 5-Factor Matching: Pairwise resource and request allocations governed by a transparent, auditable mathematical formula balancing urgency, geospatial proximity, skills, availability, and past impact;
  3. Deliberative Plan Governance: A structured 5-stage consensus lifecycle enforcing categorized critiques and immutable decision logging; and
  4. Living Institutional Memory: Automatic outcome codification that transforms today's operational responses into tomorrow's disaster preparedness knowledge;

the project establishes an open, reproducible, and robust paradigm for digital public infrastructure. Powered by modern web technologies—React 19, Node.js, Express 5, Better-SQLite3, and Leaflet—the system achieves high performance, sub-millisecond query execution, and zero operational licensing overhead.

Future Scope & Roadmap

The architecture lays a solid foundation for continuous evolution across three strategic phases:

  • Phase 1 (Near-Term): Offline P2P Mesh Synchronization: Integrating Conflict-Free Replicated Data Types (CRDTs) and WebRTC data channels to enable peer-to-peer browser synchronization across local ad-hoc Wi-Fi networks when cellular backhauls are severed.
  • Phase 2 (Mid-Term): Cryptographic Provenance & Decentralized Identifiers (DIDs): Incorporating W3C Decentralized Identifiers and Verifiable Credentials to allow medical personnel and certified responders to cryptographically sign disaster telemetry.
  • Phase 3 (Long-Term): Federated Inter-Community Protocol: Engineering an ActivityPub-inspired federated protocol allowing independent municipal mesh instances to securely share surplus relief inventories across regional borders.
